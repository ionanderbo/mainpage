01 · About & Translational Vision

I want to build the device, not only read about it.

My goal is to develop medical hardware that solves hard clinical problems. To get there, I pair my Biomedical Engineering coursework at the University of Deusto with the parts that turn an early prototype into a real clinical product: securing intellectual property, understanding patent claims, and studying how international venture capital backs life sciences.

Preparing to work across European research ecosystems is part of my weekly routine. Alongside full-time engineering classes, I dedicate six hours every week to mastering German (targeting B2/C1) so I can execute my Bachelor’s Thesis (TFG) inside Germany’s or the UK’s leading biomedical hubs.

On campus, I serve as a student representative and volunteer as an on-call speaker during university open days—explaining biomedical hardware and clinical engineering pathways to prospective students and their families.
