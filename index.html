3D SlicerPythonRadiomicsIntroduction to Biomedical Engineering

Lung nodule characterisation and CT-guided biopsy planning

With three teammates I analysed a chest CT in 3D Slicer: segmented a solid nodule, built a 3D model, planned a virtual needle path, registered a displaced scan back to the original, and extracted radiomic features.

The plan crosses the pleura once and avoids ribs and vessels. The radiomics look benign on the images, but I learned why the numbers alone are not conclusive.

What I learned about the limits

The mean intensity of −171.8 HU looks far too low for soft tissue. It reflects a rim of aerated lung inside my segment (17.5% of voxels below −500 HU), not the density of the lesion. Sphericity of 0.77 was pulled down by 2.5 mm slices and by the nodule resting on the diaphragm. The software gives numbers easily; interpreting them means knowing what produced them.

At about 18 mm, the nodule is above the 8 mm Fleischner threshold for further work-up, which supports planning a biopsy. Next steps would be respiratory motion, non-rigid registration and AI-assisted segmentation.

EEG signal quality and artifact analysis

In a signals lab I checked sampling and aliasing, ranked 14 channels by contact quality, band-pass filtered between 1 and 40 Hz, and classified artifacts such as eye blinks using the accelerometer.

Dropping the worst channel, P7, raises usable recording epochs from 26.60% to over 70%.
