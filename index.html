About me

I want to build the device, not just read about it.

I’m drawn to the hands-on side of medical technology: designing hardware that solves a clear clinical problem, testing its limits, and understanding how an engineering prototype actually survives regulation, patents, and hospital adoption.

Alongside my classes in Bilbao, I put six hours every week into learning German (aiming for B2/C1) so I can join a biomedical engineering lab in Germany or the UK for my fourth-year thesis (TFG)—focusing on surgical robotics, smart implants, or diagnostic devices.

At Deusto, I also serve as a student representative and speak at university open days to share what studying Biomedical Engineering really looks like.
